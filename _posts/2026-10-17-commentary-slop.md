# LibSlop Commentary

A couple times a year, especially when I had a little more tired of slightly more sophisticated content, I tend sink into the ocean of commentary YouTube. At first, the videos look okay, somehwat informative, let you know what kind of stuff your peers are living with. Then, suddenly, you are hit with the realization that you have been watching absolute slop. People who take some topic that is bad and then basically say "Look at how bad this thing that is bad is. Look how good I am for saying that this bad thing that is bad is, indeed bad." Becoming aware of this is very daunting, especially when I start applying higher standards to the content I consume and, by proxy, start applying higher standards to the things that I produce - my philosophy, my blog entries, etc. 

In this day and age, it is extremely difficult to produce (*AND consume*) something novel and informative. People usually work for years to produce high impact scientific articles and half-assed philosophical articles are extremely easy to spot once you know what to look out for. One time I was reading this philosophical/sociological essay and saw that it was basically a regurgitation of 3 cited authors with only some "novel" conclusions which in essence simply rephrased the theses of those authors. Of course, I am not saying that everything that gets produced needs to be novel, high-impact, and make you think. I am an enjoyer of simple art - simple landscape pictures, nice whimsical posters, and even the occassional slop youtube video. The problem is that this stuff enters your world very easily and then takes over it extremely easily. After one sloptube video, it is very easy to click on the next one. This is contrary to, for example, reading materialist/communist literature or physics articles. I have a goal of reading at least 20 oages of communist literature a week, which means most books I read take me weeks to months to finish. And once I am done with one, I am not particularly eager to go to the next one despite there being so many things I want to read on. Additional communist reading is so easy to replace with light-weight slop content. This is where the problem arises. 

Again, I know that writing this I have not said anything particularly new or interesting. I did not even bless the reader with a single citation of some niche author whom "I have been reading" because I haven't. Thinking is... hard. And this is exactly why it is so easy to fall into anti-intellectualism. Often, my first reaction to something I disagree with is "it is not that deep". However, I feel very uncomfortable with that conclusion. Maybe it is that deep and I am just stupid/ignorant/reactionary? It feels like there is always more thinking to be done, more books to be read, and more things to be learned. It is very tempting, however, to just stop and do something easier. I have been consistently reading my 20+ pages a week and yet I do not recall the last time I learned a new word. 

To be fair, this blog for me is a little bit of a tool to synthesize my thoughts and knowledge in a more coherent manner. Since my knowledge is not particularly special or novel, I cannot expect that the synthesis of it will be. Nevertheless, I would like for it to be something interesting, perhaps an unorthodox lens or an idea that has not yet been popularized to dirt. Thinking and reading is all I have been doing in the past couple months and it has made me an even more insufferable conversationalist. I cannot help but think about "epistemology" and "the anti-intellectualism epidemic". Before leaving to lectures this morning, I was watching a video criticising pseudo-intellectual video essays and it used a clip stating that epistemology is an oppressive western construct. I did not get the chance to hear out the elaboration of the statement but it caught my attention, especially as a thesis used to defend anti-intellectual behaviour (I rewatched the video after coming home and there was NO elaboration). One comment on the video said "in English literature, we were always taught to summarize the main ideas in the first paragraph and then analyze. Most video essayists, however, only summarize." And I think this might be the problem. It is like me saying "This thing is bad because capitalism." While I assume that my reader is aware of basic materialist theory and understands my implicit premises, I still try to elaborate a little bit, be intellectually honest about my statements. 

When it comes to availability, acquisition, creation, and retention of knowledge, the question of privilege and material conditions is unavoidable. It starts from the most basic concepts like scarcity mentality and personal dignity and goes down very deep to the thought patterns and conditioning of marginalized individuals. My mother is a highschool physics teacher (I guess physics is just in our genetics haha), and she told me she had a girl who came up to her and asked "Are girls allowed to participate in the physics olympiads?" In the year of our lord 2026! She said she almost cried when she heard the question. That same week I was tutoring a student who was reluctant to choose the international baccalaureate program because her teacher told her that she is not good enough to be able to handle the workload and comprehend the material (I told here: "please do not allow one teacher's words to scare you away from making the right decision for you.", and she was doing really well during the lesson so I did not lie just for the sake of encouragement). These self-insufficiency ideas are not foreign to me as well. In the past, I have mentioned that my confidence in my intellectual capabilities was certainly affected by the way my teachers saw me and the ideas they were propagating. I had one well-meaning mathematics teacher who told me to be careful about pursuing academics because it requires a lot of sacrifice, both personal and familial. She said this to me as a PhD in mathematics, so I knew she was speaking from personal experience. I went into physics partly because I felt I was "too stupid for math" and even wanted to switch to medicinal chemistry as it seemed like something more tangible and easier to understand. I am not in any way stating that physics is inferior to math or that chemistry is inferior to physics. It has much more to do with the level of abstraction necessary for each field. And I guess I implicitly believed that girls are not capable of "higher" levels of abstraction. The only reason I ended up sticking with physics was because I knew I could not forgive myself if I hadn't tried to pursue it at all. This is all to say that I have had a taste of what it is like to be doubted by others and in turn starting to doubt yourself.

It is absolutely ridiculous that people in less privileged positions have to exert much more determination and perserverance than more privileged people to achieve the same goals but it has to be done. It is much more difficult for them to avoid the slop. I fight the slop in my life every day, I try to motivate it by "my inherent predisposition to liking friction", and I applaud myself every time I choose to sit down and read something instead of resorting to this, for lack fo a better word, somewhat primitive entertainment. On the weekends, instead of a video essay about how some new celebrity culture thing is bad, I now reward myself with an interesting movie. A couple weeks ago I watched *Blind Willow, Sleeping Woman* based on Haruki Murakami's stories (I **love** Haruki Murakami!) and last week I watched *Good Bye, Lenin!* - two very different but absolutely amazing movies which gave me more stuff to talk about with my partner than any sloptube video essay could ever have. The movies made me slightly more interesting as a person and I think that is the goal. Avoiding slop is important because it allows us to be interesting and to produce interesting things. 

These small but consistent mental efforts, I know, will be extremely good for me in the long-term but it is a fight. A fight between the slop worm and me. And I am determined to win.

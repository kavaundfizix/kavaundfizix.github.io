# Underground Communist Yakub

Because of my focus on studying and reading since I was very young, I ended up not being a particularly social person. Even now, after several years of learning how to be better at regular conversations and not panic when I need to have a conversation with a person whom I don't know or make a phone call, I still have a long way to go. While I have become more outgoing and now am able to make a decent first impression (if we ignore the first first impression of being greeted by an rbf), I rarely form even acquaintance-like friendships, let alone genuine friendships. And my biggest problem? I have no decorum and, unfortunately, it is not for lack of trying. 

I am one of those people where my friends tell me "at first I thought you were scary/mean, but turns out you are actually pretty nice!" It makes a lot of science. Being social, even to small extents, drains my energy and I like to focus my energy on studying. I also have problems making small talk. I know it is a crucial part of getting to know a person and inviting a more meaningful conversation but I just can't do it. So, I end up saying things that may sound crude or rude or just somehwat inappropriate for the context, which, as you might imagine, does not appear particularly inviting. 

This has especially been problematic because of how much I have read about certain things and how excited I am to share them with people. In my closest relationships, I always welcome it when my bs is called out. It is one of the things that I value most in my relationships. Everyone can be nice to you and then stab you in the back but the real ones will always talk nice behind your back and tell you the truth without sugarcoating in private. 

My biggest problem is not being a Mamdani. I feel like the Venn diagram of nice people and people who get stuff done is basically 2 circles with infinitesimal overlap. Nice people usually irritate me because they are so sweet and then eventually they either turn out not so nice or they are incapable of keeping commitments and unreliable. People who get stuff done are usually at least a little repulsive in the way they talk to other people. I am aware that this is an over-generalization (perhaps, a hasty one), but so far it has not been falsified in my experience. 

Mamdani somehow manages to sit in that infinitesimal overlap. His likeability makes me want to sit for hours and analyze his interviews to reveal all the secrets hiding behind that unassuming teddy-bear-like smile. He is disgustingly likeable and always looks presentable. It is interesting to see how he has become more of an aspiration to me than Doctor House. Sometimes I really think that being a crazy genius with drug problems and no stable relationships might not be the lifestyle I want but I do not know if I want to be as nice as Mamdani either. 

Every time I try to have a conversation with a person who is not a close friend, each word and phrase I say is somehwat calculated and I have to juggle a lot of mental math. "Does this sound bad to a person who doesn't know me?", "Have I asked enough questions or did I only answer theirs?", "How can I relate to this story they just told me?" ... It is honestly exhausting and at times frustrating. I just cannot fathom how such a superficial beginning can lead to a potentially deeper friendship. It feels like a waste of time. I know I am not the only one but I have not met many who struggle with conversations as much as I do. Of course, Mamadani is not friends with everyone who talks to him but he is able to appear as one. I find it hard to believe that this is something that can be learned. He had to have been born with it. 

Sometimes I want to be likeable. It is "useful". I have watched my partner very skillfully making conversations with the most random people and them leaving the conversation thinking my partner is just the most based person ever. I know that it is something that he has learned but implementing his knowledge in my conversations has been extremely hard. I either just get disgusted with myself for being so ingenuine or I overthink every thing that I have said that might have been overshared in the hopes of getting the other person to open up. I talk about being good at conversations when I cannot even make eye contact properly! 

I will say that I find public speaking much easier and much more fun. I am able to separate the public judgement of me as a performer and me as a person particularly well. If they did not like my speech, they did not like my speech. It is not a reflection of me as a person. Moreover, speeches have so much more control and rehearsal. My main goal is just to memorize a couple of key lines and know my audience and suddenly I am a completely different person. This reminds me how I went to a bar karaoke event alone this summer to expose myself to social anxiety. I went on that karaoke stage twice. And was the first one to go. And supported all the other people with applause and encouraging nods. However, once the event was over, I put my head down and basically sprinted out of the place and was glad that I will not have to show up there ever again. And I did well, I know my singing was not the worst that evening. It's funny how the human mind works. But again, Milda, the performer, and Milda, the person, are two completely different entities. I just find it hard to turn on Milda, the performer, in one-on-one or group conversations which are unrehearsed, unpredictable, and sometimes people might misinterpret me or get offended just because I might have looked at them weird.

Honestly, this is one of the things where I do not have a reasonable solution. It has been sisyphian and exhausting. Rant over, I will now go back to my inactivity in solving this and just say that Mamdani was genetically engineered in a lab.

# "It is just like riding a bike"

Yesterday, I decided to play a chess game with my partner. I used to be pretty consistent at beating him at the game but... Apparently I have lost my form quite a bit. I could not see the board the way I used to be able to previously and my moves were more "let's hope this works" rather than "I know this is a mate in 3". And there is good reaosn for it. In the past couple of years I have really abandoned chess. I only started playing when I was 15, got somewhat decent at it (never played competitively, of course), and then played it during lunch breaks in highschool with some of my classmates. Once I got to university, however, I did not have as many opportunities to play with actual people. I believe there is not even a chess club in my university but also I did not have time to attend one, even if I there was. 

In this final year of my undergrad studies, I have started focusing much more on *productive* hobbies which enrich my brain and give me "good" kind of dopamine instead of the "evil" kind. And I do quite a lot. In fact, writing this blog is one of those hobbies. I also do calisthenics, study Spanish, read non-fiction, crochet, and write a Lithuanian textbook. Obviously, my priority is studying so I can only dedicate so much time to actually doing those hobbies and adding another one to the list feels like... a list. A to-do list rather than an enjoyable activity list. However, it is a good pastime activity - it makes you strategize and make decisions under time constraints, and probably has some mysterious benefits "science says" it does. 

I guess my main concern is not to burn out while doing the things that I like. I want to continue liking them. Yet, the balance is becoming increasingly more difficult. I always have a "holy" day once a week. For me, it is Sunday. On Sunday, I do not study, I can only clean if I didn't do my cleaning on Saturday or do my hobbies. I am still on a journey to rest without feeling guilty or like I am robbing myself of some very valuable study time. I objectively know that I am not, but it still does not feel 100% right. It is a strange feeling, particularly considering how most of my peers appear to be skipping lectures and having time to go out while I am here trying to scramble up and gather the last productive minutes of my time to improve some arbitrary number on a piece of paper (gpa on my diploma). 

Many people, when they hear about my routine, most of the time have the reaction of "how do you manage to do all that?" For me, it has always mostly been about focusing on consistency and building habits that will not rely on motivation. I know, easier said than done. When it comes to this blog writing, it certainly relies more on inspiration as this is not a fixed-structure text about a particular science topic nor is it just a diary where I describe events in chronological order. Most of the time, however, an idea just pops into my mind when I sit down to write. I sit with it for a minute and the ideas flow in. I think this is a result of me doing low-stimulation activities most of the time. It makes my brain try to entertain itself in some more creative ways. 

Chess is a difficult skill to maintain. I remember playing quite a bit on chess apps but that lack of human interaction or sometimes mean interaction with the other players and the fear of my rating dropping would discourage me from playing consistently. Now, most of those apps have bots of different levels that are useful for practice and for me, again, it does not feel like a real chess game. Of course, it is and it is useful for learning if you do not have anyone to give you human feedback but there is something about playing chess in-person that just feels different. It is two minds very present in the same moment and moments of silly rivalry and feeling adrenaline rushing through your veins when you make a risky move when essentially you are *just moving pieces of wood on a board*. There is something special and exciting about that. 

I think I will try to approach chess like many other hobbies that I am revisiting. I will do it slowly and methodically, first brushing up on some openings and endings, and then doing some puzzles before I feel confident enough to play with people online. Like with many things we do in life, this should not be about focusing on "keeping a good number" but rather about enjoying the process and enriching your mind. I think this is one of the most important realizations I have made so far in my life. Just like riding a bike might be difficult if you haven't done it in years, contrary to popular opinion, chess will also take some time getting back to but I am sure I can do it with my small but consistent chunks of effort.

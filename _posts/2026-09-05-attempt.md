# The Struggle of Not Being Able to Be a Mamdani

Zohran Mamdani, who probably does not require an introduction, is the current mayor of New York City. We have all seen him in news articles and reels or interviews. One thing about him that immediately drew my attention was how charismatic and charming he was. Many people have the impression that communists/socialists/lefties (whatever you want to call them) are these insufferable little goblins repeating phrases like "the material conditions of the proletariat are the paradigm of today's society in the capitalist machine of exploitation and serfdom". I am guilty of this as well. When I talk to a new person about my political beliefs, I often find myself pouring theory on them basically immediately. My reasoning for that is to show the person that Marxism is not just "communism is when communal toothbrush" but that it has merit and science behind it. This was one of the things that attacted me to the ideology as well when I first started reading about it. 

Marxism is very scientific and despite Marx's ideas being complex and, at times, tricky to understand, the way he presents those ideas is so efficient and elegant. It is always a logical statement with boolean operators or a clear grammatical juxtaposition. However, most people have little interest in that. "Das Kapital" is a huge book (3 huge books, if we want to be particularly pedantic here) so we end up at "Socialism is when the government does stuff." 

I think a much more productive focus for us, who just don't have what it takes to be a mamdani, would be to think about the reasons behind our interest in Marxism. The reasons are pretty clear - we want people to be fed, dignified, and equal. We want the production machinery to work for us rather than us working for the machinery. Achieving that under capitalism is impossible. It is inherently unequal, exploitative, and colonial.

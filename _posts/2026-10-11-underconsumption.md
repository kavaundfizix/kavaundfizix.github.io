# Underconsumption Core

Lately, I have been seeing many videos about underconsumption and low consumption in general. This is a niche that I am very interested in as I like to lead a fairly minimalistic lifestyle and find the idea of having many things suffocating. In one of these videos, a woman was talking about the things she will not buy this autumn, one of which was seasonal drinks or, in general, any seasonal items. Weirdly enough, there were many comments saying "I agree with you on almost everything but I cannot live without my pumpkin spice latte" or "but I love fall-themed candles". Basically, people defending their consumption wants and habits under a video where a person was talking about themselves. 

The tone of the video did not strike me at all as accusatory or "you should do exactly what I do." It was more "these are the things I say no to, you might want to consider saying no to them as well." The youtuber was fairly small and respinded to all comments just saying something like "you do you girlie love that for you <333". I found this quite silly and it reminded me of the analogy of a person posting a bean soup recipe and comments saying "but i am allergic to beans". I think the video was really more meant as an invitation to think about one's consumption and some comments even shared their tips of how to make the seasonal drinks at home with syrups that are much cheaper than going to cafes or others saying that because they love fall, they allow themselves to spend more money to stock up on the scented candles and similar things but then limit their spendings for the resy of the year. 

One comment, however, said "this is so restrictive, you are allowed to want and buy things." Yes, you are and this is exactly what capitalism wants you to do.Consumption is important to keep the system running. This is particularly apparent in one of the most surprising facts that I have learned about financial crises. They arise not because of deficit as one might be inclined to believe bur because of surplus that has to be destroyed in order for the economy to recover. This is mostly because of the supply-demand business. The more of a commodity you have and the fewer consumers of said commodity there are, the lower its monetary value is. In order to bring the value back up, the surplus commodities have to be destroyed so they are in demand again. The system wants to create surplus and it wants you to consume it so people who refrain from consumption are painted as weirdos or cheapskates or just poor. I see it differently. I prefer to buy fewer things but the things I buy last me extremely long. I have 3 wool coats (that is at least 6 yards of linen!), one for spring/fall and 2 for winter (one was a hand-me-down from my sister) and I have had these particular coats for about 6 years now. Moreover, they are not showing any significant sign of wear and in my opinion look much better than the puff jackets everyone is wearing. This is the case with many of my things. Most of the "home clothing" I have was either given to me by someone else or just something so worn out I did not want to wear it in public but which still functions great as a sleeping shirt. When it comes to food, I like simple meals that are easy to make but also nutritious so rice and beans is a staple in my kitchen which I eat 3-4 days out of the week. And I am thriving. 

I understand that my good coats is a sign of privilege, the ability to buy something expensive intially which will then last me a decade and this is not the case for everyone. However, here I am focusing on the cases that can reasonably afford the 7 - 10 dollar lattes daily. Consuming might be fun and indulgent for a while but it is not sustainable financially nor environmentally. Every time I buy a new item of clothing, I just see all that landfill full of fast-fashion items. That feeling for me is much more unpleasant than refraining from additional consumption and perhaps just figuring something out with the stuff I already have. It is very fun and creative to mix and match the items you already have to create something novel and interesting. And it is much more of a challenge than just buying the outfit you had in mind with one click. 

I also think that the availability of commodities has made us much less grateful for what we have. For example, I am a sucker for Christmas cookies and baked goods. I love a good gingerbread, or the german spekulatius cookies. I went to the store at the beginning of September, the cookies were already there! I had to tell myself "No, we are not buying this until November." Are the Christmas cookies still as enjoyable as they would be if you'd been eating them since September?! That is why I feel the Christian advent period of refraining from certain indulgences is a good practice to have. The Christmas dinner is much nicer when you haven't had many treats at least a couple days prior. Birthdays are fun because they happen once a year. I am not religious but this is one thing which I think is important for us to keep to. The plain old refraining from the very accessible pleasures of life to make them more pleasurable later. 

This ties back to the dopamine conversation that is still quite prevalent in popular media. It will probably not come as a surprise to you that "fast" dopamine of scrolling and instant gratification gives a rise to a spike in our brain that can be extremely difficult to recover from and it is much better to anticipate and work to achieve the feeling of pleasure, be it reading a difficult book or enjoying Christmas cookies only starting from November (I will probably expand on the dopamine conversation in a later entry). I am not advocating for all of us becoming monks or hermits but I do believe we have thing or two to learn from them.
